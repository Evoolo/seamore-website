# Sea More Expeditions — corporate identity

**Status:** Proposed identity system, grounded in the recovered website. This is a design direction for review, not a claim that the colors and type below were previously official. See the [visual preview](identity-preview.html) and the [brand guide](BRAND_GUIDE.md).

## The idea

**The field guide to extraordinary encounters.** Sea More plans wildlife journeys around the animal or natural phenomenon a traveler hopes to experience. Its identity should feel like a knowledgeable expedition companion: observant, capable, open to wonder, and attentive to the person traveling. The founding story connects firsthand experience with greater understanding and respect for wildlife. [History](CONTENT.md#article-135) · [Why us](CONTENT.md#article-159)

**Promise:** Thoughtfully planned encounters with the natural world, with experienced people beside you.

**Personality:** Curious, assured, personal, and grounded. Never theatrical or fearless for its own sake.

**Primary audience:** Divers, snorkelers, wildlife photographers, and nature travelers. The identity must work for guided groups, tailored journeys, and private expeditions. [Group tours](CONTENT.md#article-161) · [Custom tours](CONTENT.md#article-132) · [Exclusive travel](CONTENT.md#article-133)

## Identity principles

| Principle | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Wildlife leads | Start with the animal and its environment; show the location and experience before selling accommodation or luxury. |
| Knowledge builds confidence | Use precise species names, location notes, itinerary facts, and clear descriptions of guidance. |
| Wonder stays honest | Show real field photography and natural conditions. Never imply a guaranteed sighting or a risk-free encounter. |
| Travel feels personal | Show the guide, fellow travelers, and the scale of a human in the environment when useful. |
| Choice stays visible | Explain the distinction between group, custom, and exclusive travel in every service overview. |

## Visual identity

### Logo

The [modernized full-color logo](assets/images/logo-modern.png) is the primary mark for this identity. It retains the [original logo's](assets/images/logo.png) red sail, blue wave, dark supporting shape, and full name, with simpler geometry and a contemporary wordmark. Use it on white or Salt backgrounds. On dark layouts, place it on a Salt panel until a matching reverse version is produced.

- Keep the artwork's proportions and leave clear space around it equal to at least half the visible symbol height.
- Minimum digital width: **220 px** for the full wordmark. If it must be smaller, use the name as typeset text rather than shrinking the bitmap.
- Do not recolor, stretch, add outlines or shadows, or place the black wordmark directly on a dark photograph.
- The proposed logo is a transparent raster concept (2142 × 433 px). Have a designer redraw and proof a vector master before large-format print or trademark use.

### Color system

The palette takes its dark blues from underwater photographs, pale blue from sky and surface light, and a restrained red from the existing sail mark. These are **proposed design tokens**; they are not measured specifications from the old site.

| Token | Hex | Role | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Abyss | `#071D34` | Primary | Headings, dark fields, navigation, footer |
| Deep Blue | `#0C4D78` | Secondary | Rules, section labels, data, links on light fields |
| Current | `#116E98` | Supporting | Maps, diagrams, secondary buttons |
| Glacier | `#D9EEF4` | Soft field | Information panels and quiet backgrounds |
| Salt | `#F7F9F8` | Light field | Reading surfaces and generous negative space |
| Signal Red | `#B52337` | Accent on light | Calls to action, important markers, small emphasis |
| Signal Red Light | `#FF5A61` | Accent on dark | Small text or markers on Abyss only |

Use **Salt/Abyss as the default pair**, with Deep Blue for structure. Let photographic color remain natural. Use red on no more than a few high-priority elements per page. White text on Abyss, Deep Blue, Current, and Signal Red meets a contrast ratio above 4.5:1 for normal text; Signal Red Light on Abyss does too. Do not set Signal Red (`#B52337`) text on Abyss.

### Typography

**Fira Sans Condensed** is the display face: strong, legible expedition headings and concise labels. **Fira Sans** is the reading face: itinerary detail, explanations, captions, and UI. These fonts are available in the current workspace; on other systems provide licensed font files or fall back to `Arial, sans-serif`.

| Role | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Display | Fira Sans Condensed Medium, 48–72 px desktop / 34–44 px mobile, tight but uncramped line height |
| Section heading | Fira Sans Condensed Medium, 30–40 px |
| Body | Fira Sans Regular, 17–19 px, line height 1.5–1.65 |
| Field label | Fira Sans Medium, 12–13 px uppercase, letter spacing 0.08em |
| Caption | Fira Sans Regular, 13–14 px; species, place, and photographer when known |

Headlines should be short and specific. Keep body columns around 60–75 characters wide. Use tabular figures for dates, durations, and prices. Avoid decorative script, distressed textures, and all-caps body copy.

### Photography

The existing library gives the identity its strongest proof. Use **authentic field images**, including wildlife in habitat, a human for scale, and quiet landscape transitions. The animal should have room to exist in the frame. Preserve natural blue and gray tones; allow warm light from the original scene. Do not exaggerate saturation, paste animals into new settings, or crop out photographer credits embedded in legacy images.

| Image role | Existing example | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead encounter | [Whale shark and diver](assets/images/Articles/WhaleSharks/CR_MG_2595.jpg) | Animal, scale, and open water in one frame |
| Guided experience | [Tiger shark with divers](assets/images/Articles/Tigersharks/CR_MG_8923.jpg) | Shows participation and proximity without invented drama |
| Sense of place | [Orcas in northern light](assets/images/Articles/Orcas/CRIMG_2856_Small.jpg) | Connects wildlife to a real landscape and season |
| Field practice | [Crocodile encounter](assets/images/Articles/Crocodiles/CR_MG_6408.jpg) | Shows people and the setting rather than an isolated animal |

Some site photos carry visible credits to Daniel Flormann, Camilla Figenschou, Nuno Sá, and others. Keep those credits intact and verify usage rights before new public or print use. The website export alone does not establish a license.

### Layout and graphic language

Use large, unframed photographs; plain light reading surfaces; deep-blue section breaks; and thin rules reminiscent of a field notebook. Small labels such as **SPECIES / PLACE / SEASON** organize information. A simple line or coordinate-like marker can guide scanning, but avoid faux maps, compass clutter, shark-tooth shapes, and stock adventure badges. Use sharp corners or only a subtle 4 px radius. Favor generous spacing over decoration.

## Service architecture

The offer should read in this order:

1. **Wildlife expeditions** — animal-led journeys and published group departures. Explain the animal, chosen place, conditions, guidance, and what is included.
2. **Custom journeys** — tailored itinerary, extensions, and destinations beyond the published list. Explain what support is included for that specific trip.
3. **Exclusive travel** — private participants, activities, and a broader service package arranged by request. Present it as a tailored option, not a universal inclusion list.
4. **Photography and learning** — optional guidance and courses where offered, supporting the expedition rather than replacing the wildlife story.
5. **Fine-art prints** — a secondary gallery offer, separate from travel booking.

Avoid using historical trip prices or dates as current offers. [Expeditions](CONTENT.md#mainmenu-en) · [Gallery](CONTENT.md#article-166)

## Verbal identity

Write as an experienced guide speaking to a curious traveler. Use concrete nouns and active verbs. Pair emotional appeal with practical information: **what you may encounter, why this place, who guides, what happens, what varies**.

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| “Travel to places chosen for whale shark encounters.” | “Guaranteed once-in-a-lifetime sightings.” |
| “Learn to observe sharks with experienced guides.” | “Conquer the ocean’s most dangerous predators.” |
| “Choose a group expedition or ask us to tailor the journey.” | “The ultimate package for everyone.” |

**English line:** “See more. Understand more.”  
**German line:** “Mehr sehen. Mehr verstehen.”

**Sample introduction:** “We follow the wildlife, choosing places for the encounters that matter to you. Join a guided group, shape a custom journey, or travel privately—with experienced people to help you make sense of what you see.”

## Applications

- **Website hero:** One full-width field photograph, a short animal-led headline, one sentence of context, and a clear “Explore expeditions” action. Keep text over a solid field or a verified dark area of the image.
- **Expedition page:** Species and location first; then season, activity, group size or format, guidance, inclusions, and an honest note about conditions and sightings.
- **Trip card:** Photograph, species, place, travel format, duration, and an inquiry action. Use a small red marker only for the action or a genuinely important status.
- **Brochure:** A large encounter photograph on the cover, detailed field notes inside, clear photographer credits, and practical trip facts in a consistent grid.
- **Social:** One real encounter or field moment per post, a specific caption, species/place credit, and restrained branding. No sensational predator language.

## Governance

This system is a proposed modernization of the existing identity. Before public rollout, confirm current services, destinations, certifications, prices, legal claims, and image rights with Sea More. Then create vector logo masters, package the selected fonts, and update website and print templates from these tokens.
